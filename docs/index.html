Question 1

Why H33 got 0.2778, the best sibling score

The score is the user-reported score for h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros. H33 is D2.8 with every dot within 200 m of a catalogue fault deleted, leaving 37,654 dots on the H19-5 topographic ridges. D2.8 itself is 44,090 dots placed every 2.8 px along those ridges.

Three things explain the score. Two come from the metric and one from geology.

  • The ridges are where the new faults are. The receipts imply that about 95% of the public truth lies within 3 px of the H19-5 ridges.
  • Thin dots beat full lines. At 2.8 px spacing each dot keeps about 78% of the line credit while cutting false positives by 2.75×. That is why H19-5 at 0.192 rose to 0.260 once dotted.
  • The catalogue collar is empty. The pixel-exact mask removes known faults, and the receipts show the 200 m collar holds almost no new truth. The 100,000-dot tilt-ring raster, entirely within 2 px of the catalogue, scored 0.0005. Deleting the collar therefore removes pure false positives.

Metric algebra on the D2.8 → H33 pair, per unit of public fraction:

  • TP ≈ 5.2k and |G| ≈ 13.4k px, so H33 captures about 39% of the weighted truth.
  • These numbers predict 0.187 for the full H19-5 ridges. The user-reported score is 0.1922.
