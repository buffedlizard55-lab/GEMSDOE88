Why user-reported H33 (0.2778) leads — and whether it can be beaten

Measured on the files: H33 is a strict subset of SOLO-D2.8, of D2.8 and of H19-5 — every step in the lineage only removed dots (121,131 → 37,654), and the 6,436 dots removed from D2.8 all sat within 2 px of a mapped fault. Its dots are laid about one kernel radius apart (nearest-neighbour median 3.0 px). Inferred (owner-reported scores, pure-false-positive assumption): the public test holds ≈13–15 k fault pixels and an average H33 dot is worth ≈0.14, only ≈2.6× the 0.0556 break-even.

Best predictor of the reported ordering: share of dots >25 px from the catalogue, Spearman +0.90 (n=20). It is a necessary screen, not a selector. SGMC-based proxies are anti-correlated in the top regime (−0.93) and catalogue hide-and-recover cannot score H33-class rasters at all.
