Why H33 scored well—and why the next idea must be different

Local file forensics show that H33 is a strict dot-subset of earlier H19-5 → D2.8 → SOLO outputs: the later steps only removed predictions. H33 dots are approximately one 300 m kernel radius apart, and removals from D2.8 were concentrated within 2 pixels of the mapped-fault catalogue. This is consistent with the official metric's max-credit/false-positive trade-off. The observed pixel geometry is measured; causal attribution of the reported score increase to those removals is an inference because the scores were owner-reported, not organizer receipts.

What the competition actually asks

The official problem is discovery of geological faults indicative of geothermal resources; it does not ask us to predict vents directly. Predictions are a single-band probability/confidence GeoTIFF in the training grid and values must lie between zero and one. The official metric uses a triangular 300 m kernel and weights false positives by 0.2 and false negatives by 0.8.
