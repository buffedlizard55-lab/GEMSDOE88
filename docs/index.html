Forensic analysis · why H33 leads sibling list @ 0.2778 (user-reported)

Why H33's owner-reported 0.2778 is plausible — and why it doesn't prove a leaderboard win

What H33 is (pixel-forensic, read-only)

Decoding SHA-pinned GEMSDOE25 D2.8 (44,090 dots) and GEMSDOE32 H33 (37,654 dots, SHA c55bafc…) against existing_faults.tif (60,988 public fault pixels) proves mathematically:

supp(H33) ⊂ supp(D2.8), |D2.8 \ H33| = 6,436, and ∀x∈Δ: d_cat(x) ≤2 px. Every dot H33 retained beyond 200 m of mapped faults (68.4% at d_cat>10 px, 41.8% at d_cat>25 px) is pixel-identical to D2.8.

Under official DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w) with 300 m triangular kernel:

  • Precision / flank-dedup: Deleting 6,436 near-catalogue flank dots (d_cat ≤2 px) eliminates up to 0.2×6,436 = 1,287 denominator FP penalty units wherever new expert faults don't parallel old traces at <200 m offset. That maps to D2.8 0.2600 → H33 0.2778 user-reported (+0.0178).
  • Off-catalogue piedmont vs halo collapse: Later unconstrained tree models placed 84–99% dots within 1 km of mapped faults (memorizing halos), starving piedmonts/basins (0.0–0.5% beyond 2.5 km). Genuine out-of-fold models place 23–68% beyond 1 km.
  • Continuous vs binary: Continuous [0,1] surfaces pay 0.2×FP across millions of non-fault cells (17GEMSDOE covers 5.2M pixels), collapsing DTI; discrete {0,1} support via Dinkelbach is strictly superior.
