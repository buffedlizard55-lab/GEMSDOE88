Question 1 · Exact Metric Mechanics

Why `GEMSDOE32` (`H33`) Outscored `GEMSDOE16..40` (`0.2778`)

The competition scores binary/continuous predictions $P \in [0,1]$ against hidden expert-labeled new faults $G$ (with mapped training faults $K$ masked out of both numerator and denominator) using Distance-Weighted Tversky ($\alpha=0.2$, $\beta=0.8$, $R=300\text{ m}=3\text{ px}$, $k(d)=\max(1-d/3,0)$):

DTI(S) = T(S) / [ 0.8 · |G| + 0.2 · (T(S) + |S \ K|) ]

  1. Binary $\{0, 1\}$ optimality (Dinkelbach LP): Because DTI is linear-fractional in $P_i \in [0, 1]$, every global optimum is strictly binary $\{0, 1\}$. Continuous probability rasters pay $0.2 P_i$ across millions of background cells for near-zero kernel gain.
  2. Exact subset proof of `H33`: Byte-level raster diffs prove H33 (37,654 dots, owner-reported 0.2778) is a strict subset of H19-5 (121,131 dots, 0.1618) → D2.8 (44,090 dots, 0.2660) → SOLO-D2.8 (40,199 dots, 0.2712):
    • H33 stripped all 83,477 collar dots inside d_cat ≤ 2.0 px (min d_cat = 2.236 px), eliminating massive $0.2|S \setminus K|$ false-positive bleed adjacent to already-masked traces.
    • H33 placed 41.7% of its dots at d_cat > 25 px (>2.5 km from known faults), vs <9% for near-ribbon siblings that collapsed to 0.09–0.12 on hidden test labels despite scoring 0.18–0.19 on hide-and-recover holdout.
  3. Why hide-and-recover holdout systematically over-rewards near-ribbon models: Withholding 20% of mapped segments leaves 86.4% of withheld fault pixels inside d_vis ≤ 25 px of surviving segments (only 3,117 / 22,893 = 13.6% lie in the 51.2% of the AOI beyond 25 px — a 10.7× density skew). Private test faults, by contrast, are genuinely unmapped basin/piedmont/relay structures.
