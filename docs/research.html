Why GEMSDOE32 (H33, user-reported 0.2778) beat older siblings — and why 0.4413 is higher

Decoding the pinned rasters across GEMSDOE32, GEMSDOE36, GEMSDOE70, GEMSDOE73, and existing_faults.tif reveals four clear empirical regimes across the owner’s ~55 user-reported submissions (unverified without private DrivenData portal receipts):

  • Regime I (0.2478–0.2778 · Sparse binary {0, 1} spaced multi-physics dots): D2.8 (44,090 dots, 0.2600) → H27-4 (40,199 dots, −3,891 at d ≤ 1 px, 0.2708) → H33 (37,654 dots, −2,545 at 1 < d ≤ 2 px, 0.2778); GEMSDOE36 (38,854 dots, 0.2750); GEMSDOE70 (37,654 submodular OOF dots, 0.2478).
  • Regime II (0.1380–0.1976 · Soft/dense surfaces or partial physics): GEMSDOE73 H73-MCSC pure Blakely–Simpson ridges (0.1917); 17GEMSDOE dense probability grid (5,167,373 positive cells, 0.1380 — crushed by 0.2 × FP_w).
  • Regime III (0.0053–0.0680 · Pure catalogue-halo / relay / tip extrapolation): GEMSDOE45 (0.0106), 57GEMSDOE (0.0254), GEMSDOE69 (0.0053) — mapped-trace survivorship bias misses blind intra-basin GeoDAWN/3DEP corridors.
  • Regime IV (0.0000–0.0044 · Over-thresholded pinpricks): 0.8 × FN_w dominates when |P| ≪ |G|.

Why H33 plateaued 0.1635 below 0.4413

Let T be truth-to-prediction max-kernel credit, M prediction-to-truth max-kernel credit, S emitted mass, and K true fault pixels:

DTI = T / [0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8 K]

H33 solved two problems — binary {0, 1} ~2.85 px kernel spacing and removing 6,436 redundant d ≤ 2 px catalogue-collar dots — but still relied on mapped-catalogue distance features (starving blind basins >2 km from mapped traces), scalar gradients (firing on circular volcanic plugs), and greedy disk NMS rather than fractional DTI optimization. STR-88 addresses all three without touching existing_faults.tif as an input feature.
