The owner-hosted GEMSDOE32 page identifies H33 and describes deleting close-to-catalogue dots. We separately decoded the pinned H33 raster (SHA-256 c55bafc47005…), the D2.8 raster, and the public fault raster on the same grid:

Why sparsification is rational but not magical

Let T be truth-to-prediction max-kernel credit, M the prediction-to-truth max-kernel credit, S total emitted mass and K the number of true pixels. With α+β=1:

DTI = T / [0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8 K]

Deleting a low-credit cluster reduces the denominator through S − M, but can reduce T too. For a single new nonredundant unit dot with own and marginal credit both k, the denominator changes by exactly 0.2; the dot improves DTI iff k > 0.2 × current DTI. This is a derivation from the organizer formula, not a proxy score. Unknown hidden labels prevent an H33 attribution.
