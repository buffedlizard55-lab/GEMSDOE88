Pixel audit · no hidden labels used

H33: what is verifiable?

The owner-hosted GEMSDOE32 page identifies H33 and describes deleting close-to-catalogue dots. We separately decoded the pinned H33 raster (SHA-256 c55bafc47005…), the D2.8 raster, and the public fault raster on the same grid:

  • D2.8 has 44,090 positive pixels; H33 has 37,654.
  • H33 is an exact subset of D2.8. Precisely 6,436 dots were deleted, and every deleted dot lies at Euclidean pixel distance ≤ 2 from mapped faults; not one retained/distant dot moved.
  • Neither artifact places any dot on a catalogue pixel. H33 has no dots at distance ≤ 2; it still has 2,171 dots at distance ≤ 3.

The public page itself called its H33 artifact unscored when published; the inherited prompt now supplies the figure 0.2778 as a user-reported outcome. No submission-page receipt was available, so we cannot certify it as an organizer score or causally estimate the value of the 6,436 removals.

Why sparsification is rational but not magical

Let T be truth-to-prediction max-kernel credit, M the prediction-to-truth max-kernel credit, S total emitted mass and K the number of true pixels. With α+β=1:

DTI = T / [0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8 K]

Deleting a low-credit cluster reduces the denominator through S − M, but can reduce T too. For a single new nonredundant unit dot with own and marginal credit both k, the denominator changes by exactly 0.2; the dot improves DTI iff k > 0.2 × current DTI. This is a derivation from the organizer formula, not a proxy score. Unknown hidden labels prevent an H33 attribution.

Why vents ≠ faults — and where blind systems hide

The competition scores faults indicative of geothermal resources, not vents. See full 5-hypothesis dossier for audited sources on each claim.

What INGENIOUS actually targets

Walker Lane / western Great Basin blind systems have no surface hot spring; the fluid pathway is a fault zone under alluvium. INGENIOUS (DOE-GDR 1391, CC BY 4.0) lists fault traces, paleogeothermal deposits, 2 m probe temps, conductance and seismicity as regional context — not as fault labels. A thermal anomaly without structural continuity is a mimic.

Three contrarian directions others overlook

  • Offset, not co-location: CGO tests 100–300 m gravity vs conductance displacement (dip, brine drift) — opposite to “bright ridge” bias.
  • Relief-stratified radiometrics: RDC conditions U/Th disequilibrium on slope/elevation deciles + strike coherence, rescuing shallow radiometrics under cover.
  • Strain-locked curvature pairs: TSC demands opposite-sign curvature transverse to InSAR shear, rejecting Lahontan shorelines that fool plain curvature.

All three use only the 19 provided bands; no new licence or external download needed. Next session tests CGO first — the best benefit/cost.
