Magnetic residual-ribbon coherence

MRR-COH-88 tests whether narrow, axially persistent demagnetized ribbons reveal buried damage corridors after surface expression is erased. Its unique production raster is useful as a negative research result; it is not safe to submit.

Mechanism and construction

What was actually tested

  1. Residualize without catalogue geometry

    Estimate the broad local VGM–TMI relation over a 10 km neighbourhood and retain its residual.

  2. Build eight feature channels

    Use negative residual troughs, axial coherence at 200/400/600 m, persistence across scales, and horizontal-gradient suppression. Every channel was tested alone.

  3. Hide buffered whole components

    Eight spatial folds withhold connected catalogue components plus a buffer. The selected eight channels contain no catalogue geometry; only visible fault pixels supply training labels, and withheld components never enter training.

  4. Produce strict spatial OOF scores

    A four-quadrant out-of-fold model places dots only after the pre-placement surface-correlation stop passes.

Forensic interpretation

Why H33 may have reached owner-reported 0.2778

The repository's byte-level reconstruction links H33 to an H19-5/D2.8 ridge family and shows it as a strict subset. In the owner-reported lineage, thinning roughly 121,000 densely redundant ridge pixels to 44,090 changes nearest-neighbour spacing from about 1 px to 3 px while the median catalogue distance stays near 15 px; the reported value rises from 0.1922 to 0.2600. The next step to H33 keeps 37,654 pixels, raises median catalogue distance from 15.0 to 19.7 px, and lowers the share within 3 px of the catalogue from 19.5% to 5.8%; its reported value rises to 0.2778.

This is compatible with the metric. The 300 m triangular support means dots separated by about 3 px own less-redundant kernel area, while the Tversky denominator charges false-positive mass (α=0.2, β=0.8). Removing redundant and near-catalogue dots can therefore improve precision enough to offset lost recall. Across 13 restored claim-linked rasters, Spearman correlation between median catalogue distance and claimed score is 0.891, but n=13 and lineage/live-feedback confounding make that descriptive, not causal.

The limitations are decisive: H33's recipe and authenticated receipt are absent; lineage scores are claims; and H33 scores about 0.004 on this hide-and-recover instrument because it was built from the full catalogue, so its live claim and the local holdout are not directly comparable. Therefore 0.2778 remains owner-reported, not HOLDOUT-DTI and not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED. It must not be substituted for the 0.187888 same-split holdout incumbent.

Can MRR-COH-88 likely beat H33?

No. Its HOLDOUT-DTI is 0.0770 with upper 95% bound 0.0849. Both are far below the 0.1879 local incumbent, let alone the unverified 0.2778 report. No honest projection from this evidence supports slot promotion.

That conclusion does not prove H33 itself scores 0.2778, nor does it prove no future method can beat it. It says this candidate is not shown likely to do so.

Limits and next work

What remains uncertain

Limitations

  • Owner-mirrored competition inputs are SHA-pinned, but independent organizer authentication was unavailable.
  • Only catalogue positives are observable; some apparent false positives may be real unmapped structures.
  • Eight spatial folds cannot represent every geological domain shift.
  • Uniqueness is measured against the accessible registry, not every private or future raster.
  • The dense-witness overlap irregularity requires a declared representation-aware policy.
  • No organizer submission was made, so local validity cannot establish portal acceptance.
