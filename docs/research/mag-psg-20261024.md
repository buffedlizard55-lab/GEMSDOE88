## Hypothesis

**MAG-PSG:** Deep basement normal faults (60° dip) buried under 0.5–2 km Cenozoic alluvium have no LiDAR scarp, but their basement density/susceptibility step persists in potential-field data. RTP magnetic anomaly senses shallow volcanic noise (short λ) that masks deep contacts. Pseudogravity transform `F_psg = F(RTP)/|k|` acts as a low-pass integrator converting dipole magnetic field to monopole-like pseudogravity, emphasizing deep, long-wavelength basement sources over shallow volcanic short-wavelength noise. Tilt angle `θ = atan(VDR/THDR)` on pseudogravity has zero-crossings precisely at deep contact edges; combining RTP demagnetization troughs (hydrothermal alteration destroys magnetite) with pseudogravity THDR ridges should localize the surface projection of blind faults.

**Layers (verified, no catalogue leakage):**
- B2 `rtp` — Reduced-to-pole magnetics (latitude-corrected) — primary input for FFT
- B13 `iso_grav_anom` — Isostatic gravity anomaly — corroborating basement density step
- B15 `depth_to_base_surf` — Depth to basement — stratifies weighting shallow vs deep regime
- B4 `geod_2ndinv` — Geodetic second invariant — strain context (not used in final surface but available)
- B18 `iso_grav_anom_hg` — Isostatic gravity horizontal gradient — unsigned step magnitude

All derived via FFT and Sobel without any `existing_faults.tif` distance column.

**Mechanism (math):**
1. Pseudogravity: `G_psg = ℱ⁻¹{ ℱ(RTP) / |k| }`, `k = 2π·f`, `f = fftfreq(N, 100m)`, DC=0. Realized via `np.fft.fft2/ifft2`.
2. Tilt: `θ = atan2(VDR_psg, THDR_psg)`, where `VDR_psg = ℱ⁻¹{ℱ(G)·|k|} = RTP` (theorem) and `THDR_psg = sqrt((∂G/∂x)² + (∂G/∂y)²)` via `ℱ(G)·i·kₓ` / `i·k_y`.
3. Surface: `S = w_trough·(1−norm(RTP)) + w_tilt·exp(−θ²/12°²) + w_thdr·norm(THDR_psg) + w_iso·norm(|∇ iso_grav|)`, where weights `w_thdr` increase with `depth_to_base/2500` (deep = more THDR) and `w_trough` decreases.
4. Greedy Poisson-disk NMS at 2.8 px (280 m) to 37,654 dots, collar 2 px from catalogue (H33 flank-dedup lesson), exclusion of footprint edge (6 px).

**Named non-fault mimic:**
Shallow Miocene volcanic caldera margins and reversed-polarity basalt flows produce high-frequency RTP anomalies without basement offset, mimicking linear lows. GeoDAWN flight-line leveling seams (400 m line spacing documented in [USGS GeoDAWN](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and)) imprint flight-parallel edges. Both are suppressed by pseudogravity low-pass but not fully eliminated — tilt-zero on a volcanic ring fault remains a false positive.

**How it differs from existing repo:**
- No prior sibling uses frequency-domain pseudogravity `1/|k|` integration + tilt phase symmetry on RTP. `MRR-BPR` used spatial block regression of `tmi_vg` on `[1, tmi, mag_anom]`; `CGO` used raw gravity/magnetic gradients without spectral filtering; `KTH` used `K/Th` radiometric ratios; `ADHR` used 60° dip-back-projection + sinter advection + LiDAR scarps. MAG-PSG is a pure spectral deep-pass alternative.

## Why H33's 0.2778 is plausible (PhD forensic, source-verified)

**Pixel lineage (read-only audit):**
Decoding SHA-pinned `GEMSDOE25 D2.8` (44,090 dots) and `GEMSDOE32 H33` (37,654 dots) against `existing_faults.tif` (60,988 fault pixels) proves `supp(H33) ⊂ supp(D2.8)` and `|Δ| = 6,436` dots, all with `d_cat ≤2 px (200 m)` of mapped faults. 68.4% of H33 dots lie at `d_cat >10 px (1 km)` and 41.8% at `>25 px (2.5 km)` — far into unmapped piedmonts/basins — pixel-identical between D2.8 and H33.

**DTI mechanism (official formula: https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/#mathematical-representation):**
`DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)` with triangular kernel `k = max(1−d/300,0)`. Each extra dot contributes `k` to TP_w if near hidden truth, otherwise `k` deficit adds to FN and `(1−k)` to FP. Marginal inclusion threshold for isolated dot is `k > 0.2·DTI` (exact, via `vendor/gems57/metric.py::marginal_inclusion_threshold` and `emit.py::dinkelbach_allocate`).

1. **Flank dedup:** Deleting 6,436 near-catalogue flank dots eliminates up to `0.2·6,436 = 1,287` FP denominator penalty units wherever new faults don't parallel old traces at <200 m offset. Owner-reported D2.8 0.2600 → H33 0.2778 (+0.0178) is consistent with this precision gain; continuous surfaces or unconstrained halos would have kept those flank dots and paid FP across millions of non-fault cells.
2. **Halo collapse vs piedmont:** Later siblings training unconstrained trees on smooth regional fields (gravity, strain, magnetics) memorized 200–1000 m halos around `existing_faults.tif` (84–99% dots within 1 km, 0.0–0.5% beyond 2.5 km), starving unmapped range-front piedmonts. H33/CGO/ADHR place 23–68% beyond 1 km, recovering blind blind faults.
3. **Discrete optimum:** Binary {0,1} allocation via Dinkelbach fractional programming strictly outperforms continuous [0,1] surfaces that spread FP mass.

## Limitations & next steps

- Pseudogravity edge artifacts at irregular footprint boundary without taper; future test should apply cosine window and mirror padding.
- No structural prior (relay ramps, bend, damage-zone width) — MAG-PSG is purely spectral; integrating `relay_bend_anatomy` transverse curvature could add along-strike coherence.
- No field validation of magnetite destruction vs lithology — need ground-magnetic or drill-constrained susceptibilities.
- Next candidates: GGL-BH (no external data, low cost) and SEIS-RAD (needs ComCat, free official source `https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/`, check obtainability before run).
