## H33 (`0.2778`): what the bytes establish, and what they do not

The pinned H33 bytes have `37,654` positive pixels. The read-only lineage audit finds H33 is an exact positive-pixel subset of H19-5, D2.8, and SOLO-D2.8. It removes `6,436` D2.8 pixels, all within 2 pixels of the mapped catalogue; the independent collar check partitions the removed set into `3,891` at at most 1 pixel and `2,545` between 1 and 2 pixels, with none beyond 2 pixels. H33 has no positive pixels within 2 pixels of the catalogue, a measured minimum nearest-neighbour distance of `2.828` pixels, and median catalogue distance `19.647` pixels. These are **REGISTRY-MEASUREMENTS**, not score evidence; see the [lineage](../data/h33_lineage_20261028_a896e29e.json) and [independent collar cross-check](../data/h33_collar_20261028_a896e29e.json).

A scientifically plausible mechanism for the reported result is **collar pruning and spatial thinning of a pre-existing ridge hypothesis**: removing points close to known faults may reduce low-value false-positive mass and reduce redundant predictions under a distance-kernel metric, while preserving much of the broader ridge network. The alternative is equally important: the removed collar pixels could include real but unmapped splays, and deleting them could reduce recall. The metric's `300 m` triangular kernel makes redundant neighbouring predictions a plausible concern, but without hidden labels or a receipt one cannot measure which deleted points were false positives, quantify their incremental truth credit, or attribute the reported delta to a geological mechanism. The thinning also changed density/spacing, so it is not evidence that H33 directly detected a geothermal vent or fault.

**Answer to “can we beat `0.2778`?”** It is possible in principle, but this repository review cannot demonstrate it. `0.2778` is a `USER-REPORTED CLAIM`, and the new hypotheses have no holdout measurements. There is no valid score projection. Under the present literal rule, the system must stop before a nonempty unique candidate can be built; claiming a higher score or emitting a renamed/cosmetic duplicate would violate the protocol.
