## Why H33 is reported at `0.2778`—and what can be concluded

The `0.2778` attached to `h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros` is a **USER-REPORTED CLAIM**. The repository contains no submission-page receipt for it, and this session did not access the authenticated contest page. A byte-level audit of pinned owner-mirror rasters establishes ancestry, not score causality:

- D2.8 has 44,090 positive cells; SOLO-D2.8 has 40,199; H33 has 37,654. H33 is an exact subset of both parents.
- The D2.8-to-SOLO step removes 3,891 cells; SOLO-to-H33 removes another 2,545. All 6,436 cells absent from H33 are within two 100-m pixels of the mapped catalogue.
- The user's listed claims rise from 0.2600 (D2.8) to 0.2708 (SOLO-D2.8) to 0.2778 (H33). These are not paired portal receipts or a controlled causal ablation.

A plausible metric-level explanation is that thinning redundant predictions near already-mapped faults could reduce weighted false-positive mass while preserving enough distance-weighted coverage. That is consistent with the `alpha = 0.2`, `beta = 0.8` DTI protocol described in this project, but it is **not established**: a prediction removed near a mapped fault could still be close to a hidden fault, and a thinning operation can also reduce true-positive coverage. GEMSDOE36's reported lower value after adding 1,200 cells is not a controlled experiment either. We cannot infer any organizer score, causal benefit, or score forecast from the geometry alone.
