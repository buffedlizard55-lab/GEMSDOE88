## H33: what can and cannot be concluded

The stored [forensic measurements](../evidence/h33_forensics_20261024.json) support sparse subset ancestry. They do not prove why a hidden-label score changed. Under the shared binary DTI definition, with truth credit T, dot self-credit M and n dots:

`DTI = T / [0.8 |G| + 0.2 T + 0.2 (n − M)]`.

Deleting a point improves DTI only if its lost truth credit is sufficiently small relative to its removed false-positive penalty. Nearby dots can cover distinct truth pixels; sparse thinning can lose recall. Catalogue-collar deletion is not guaranteed to preserve hidden truth either. A plausible explanation for H33 is reduced redundant emission, but causality and the achievable leaderboard ceiling are unknown.

**Corrections to the archived DHTC analysis:** no hidden-truth evidence establishes H33's claimed 10–15% precision, that 47.17% of the footprint is deep alluvium, or that DHTC is necessary to reach a specified leaderboard score. Those are withdrawn as established findings. In general ΔT can exceed q when a dot covers multiple truth cells. The archive is not authoritative scientific evidence.

Can H33 be beaten? Possibly; neither the observed lineage nor catalogue recovery proves a particular improvement on undisclosed faults. No numerical projection is reported as a score.
