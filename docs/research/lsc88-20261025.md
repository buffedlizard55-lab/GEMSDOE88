## 1. The metric, read line by line from the official page

Official definition ([problem page, "Performance metric"](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/#performance-metric)), transcribed:

* `TP_w = Σ_{g∈G} max_{x: d(x,g)≤R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))` — **one credit per truth pixel**, the best prediction within `R = 300 m` (3 px), `k(d) = max(1−d/R, 0)`.
* `FP_w = Σ_{x: p(x)>0} p(x)·[1 − max_g k(d(x,g))]` — every predicted pixel is charged its value times its distance from truth.
* `FN_w = Σ_g [1 − max p·k] = |G| − TP_w`.
* `DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)` = `TP_w / (0.2·TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·|G|)`.

Organizer clarifications (forum, DrivenData staff): known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels are **masked out of scoring** ([thread 11516](https://community.drivendata.org/t/11516)); a "new fault" is **any fault pixel not already captured**, including splays, continuations and parallel strands of mapped systems ([thread 11536](https://community.drivendata.org/t/11536)); the public score **pools** all public-subset pixels into one index and the private/final rounds use the same formula ([thread 11550](https://community.drivendata.org/t/11550), [thread 11556](https://community.drivendata.org/t/11556)); data sources and coverage of the test faults are **not disclosed** ([thread 11527](https://community.drivendata.org/t/11527)).

**Consequences (derivations, checked against `vendor/gems57/metric.py`):**

1. For a binary dot with expected incremental credit `t` and expected FP charge `f`, DTI rises iff `t/(t+f) > 0.2·DTI`. At DTI ≈ 0.28 that bar is **0.056** — any pixel with roughly ≥ 2.5 % chance of a new-fault pixel within ~1 px is worth a dot, provided it is not redundant.
2. Because credit is a **max per truth pixel**, two dots closer than ~3 px share credit but both pay FP. Thinning H19-5 (121,131 dots, `0.1922` USER-REPORTED) to a ≥ 2.8 px Poisson-disc carpet (44,090 dots, `0.2600`) removed 64 % of the dots and *raised* the score — the single largest step in the lineage.
3. Dots within ≤ 2 px of the catalogue are nearly worthless on the portal: `GEMSDOE81 cont_asb` (44,090 dots at median 2.2 px) scored `0.0041` USER-REPORTED, and pruning 2,545 such dots (`h27-4` → `h33`) moved `0.2708 → 0.2778`. The known-fault *mask* covers only the catalogue pixels themselves, so their 1–2 px neighbours are full-price FPs unless a new strand is there.
4. A uniform full-footprint carpet is roughly break-even at the portal's apparent truth density; score therefore comes almost entirely from the **spatial prior** (where the 37–44 k dots go), then from **spacing economics**.

## 2. Why `h33-h33-2-b2` (37,654 dots) reached 0.2778 (USER-REPORTED) — and the ceiling of that lineage

* **Lineage, verified on SHA-256-pinned rasters:** `H19-5 (121,131) ⊃ D2.8 (44,090) ⊃ SOLO (40,199) ⊃ H33 (37,654)`; every step is a strict subset. The score climbed `0.1922 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778` by *subtracting* redundant and near-catalogue dots, not by adding knowledge.
* **Fingerprint:** `min d_cat = 2.24 px`, `median d_cat ≈ 19.6 px`, `share_gt25 = 41.7 %`, Poisson-disc spacing ≥ 2.8 px. Across the 20 owner-reported rasters with dots, a linear fit of score on `share_gt25` has slope 0.64 and leave-one-out Spearman 0.85 (`evidence/fleet_distance_prior_20261024.json`, `linear_share_gt25`); the `≤ 2 px` and `2–5 px` strata carry zero or near-zero fitted value.
* **Budget algebra (estimate, not evidence):** for a sparse binary raster `TP_w = s·(0.2n + 0.8|G|)` with `n` dots and score `s`; the lineage scores are consistent with a public truth count of order 10⁴ pixels if thinning preserved most of the credit, which would put H33's credited share near one third. Treat as an order-of-magnitude reading only.
* **Ceiling:** the lineage has been thinned to its optimum (`PRUNE`, `MASS`, `SNAP`, `SPACING` axes in `releases/` all move the blind proxy by < 0.01). Beating 0.2778 requires a **better surface**, not a better thinning of H19-5.
* **Public-leaderboard context (PUBLIC-PAGE 2026-10-25):** top `0.4413` (xiaofanhu, 29 submissions), `0.3916`, `0.3904`, then a tight cluster of 14 entries at `0.326–0.3335`. A plateau that many independent entrants reach with 7–13 submissions is the signature of a shared simple approach (most plausibly the organizer's U-Net reference solution or a known-fault-neighbourhood prediction), i.e. **a continuous probability map scores ≈ 0.33 on this metric** — above every dotted carpet in the GEMSDOE fleet. This is the most important strategic observation of this session and is recorded as a hypothesis, not a fact.

## 5. Verdict — **negative; DO NOT SUBMIT; OK to download for research**

The preregistered gate requires `share_gt25 ≥ 0.35` (H33-parity, the strongest portal-grounded prior we have). The production carpet puts 84 % of its dots within 25 px of mapped faults (median 8 px) because a classifier trained on the catalogue reproduces the catalogue's neighbourhood. The portal evidence (§1.3) says that regime scores poorly. Spending a slot on it has low probability of exceeding 0.2778 and the preregistration forbids re-emitting with a different stratification after seeing the result (that is the first task of the next session, see §7).

Can this lineage beat 0.2778? **Not shown.** The holdout cannot even rank H33 (it scores 0.0039 there because its collar excludes the hidden catalogue by construction), so no local number can be read as a projection. What *is* shown: (i) lidar + 10 m topography carry recoverable off-catalogue information (+0.019 paired), (ii) density-adaptive spacing beats uniform spacing (+0.009 paired), (iii) the resulting carpet is unique against all 60 registry rasters and format-valid.
