The [official DTI definition](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) gives distance-weighted true-positive credit with a 300 m triangle and penalizes false-positive mass with alpha 0.2 versus beta 0.8 for false negatives. Eliminating redundant near-catalogue flanks can improve a sparse raster's efficiency **if** those flanks are not needed for hidden positives. That conditional is unmeasured on the hidden test set: the change in the claimed owner score cannot establish causality, and deleting dots can also remove true positives. This repo's hide-and-recover holdout ranks H33 poorly; it cannot be equated with the public test distribution. The [dated official leaderboard snapshot](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/leaderboard/) shows public leader 0.4413 (rechecked via page 2026-10-24), but it is not a receipt for H33 or our candidate; prompt's 0.3195 is stale. **No evidence PHASE-88 beats 0.2778 or 0.4413.**

## Preregistered scientific rationale and falsification

The [official competition description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) confirms 100 m EPSG:32611 provided magnetic and isostatic gravity layers, incomplete publicly mapped faults, expert-identified hidden faults, and the 300 m DTI definition. [USGS GeoDAWN](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) describes aeromagnetics as probing subsurface structure and notes varying flight height, distinct acquisition specifications and leveling corrections. **Geological hypothesis, not source fact:** spatial mismatch of normalized, smoothed gravity and RTP magnetic gradient directions may indicate a concealed basement dislocation where density and magnetization respond to different offsets. We tested |cross product| / product of gradient lengths (perpendicular=1, parallel or antiparallel=0), smoothed the direction signal, and weighted by independently percentile-ranked gradient strength of each field. This deliberately differs from existing co-edge agreement, RTP trough, and within-magnetic residual methods; it has a clear physical weakness: perpendicular gradients are also expected at ordinary *intersections of unfaulted lithologic contacts* or survey interpolation seams. It need not detect a fault and cannot establish a geothermal vent. The target is faults indicative of geothermal resources, not observed vents.

## Submission error and constraints

The [official submission format](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) calls for one float32 band, [0,1] values, 100 m EPSG:32611 exact bounds; it describes outside-bounds cells as null/NaN. This project's all-finite zeros policy deliberately avoids the reported portal rejection “Predicted values must be in range [0, 1]”, but **we have not tested that policy against the current portal**. The writer fails on any input NaN/Inf or out-of-range value rather than clipping; the independent TIFF/ZIP check reopens bytes, matches sample CRS/shape/transform, checks no NaN inside footprint, no out-of-range pixels anywhere, zero mass outside footprint and on mapped catalogue, and checks one exact TIFF in ZIP. An owner-mirrored sample may diverge from a fresh organizer sample; authenticate original bytes before any actual upload. The rules [PDF](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy26osti/96647.pdf) may change; check live rules/portal and weekly cap only at the **separate selector stage**. The official format says predict **all** faults, while the hidden initial round tests missing faults; zeroing known faults is a research design choice, not proven compliant with final-round rescoring. Flag for review, not silently resolved.
