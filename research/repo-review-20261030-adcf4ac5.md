## What H33-B2's reported 0.2778 might mean

The byte-level lineage gives a plausible mechanism, not causal proof. In the pinned historical files, D2.8 has 44,090 binary dots; SOLO-D2.8 has 40,199; H33-B2 has 37,654. H33-B2 is a strict subset of both. It removes 6,436 D2.8 dots, all within two 100-m pixels of the known catalogue (3,891 within one pixel and 2,545 in the 1–2 pixel band). Under the user-provided metric description—distance-weighted Tversky, false-positive weight α=0.2, false-negative weight β=0.8, 300-m triangular kernel—removing predictions whose kernel credit is redundant near known traces could reduce false-positive cost while preserving coverage. This is a reasonable explanation for why pruning might help *within that family*.

What the evidence does **not** show: it does not show those 6,436 dots were false positives against private labels; it does not isolate pruning causally; it does not validate 0.2778 from an organizer receipt; it does not show H33-B2 beats a new independent model; and it cannot be converted to an expected score. Later holdout checks of a different candidate/population do not validate the original reported score. The owner-reported sequence (0.2477 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778) is confounded by nested support and experiment differences.
