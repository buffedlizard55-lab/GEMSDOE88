## 2. H33 (`0.2778`) — plausible mechanism versus proof

### What the bytes establish

The dated owner-mirror lineage and an independent disk-dilation audit establish the following **REGISTRY-MEASUREMENTS**, not leaderboard performance:

- D2.8 contains 44,090 positive cells; SOLO-D2.8 contains 40,199; H33 contains 37,654.
- H33 is a pixel-support subset of both D2.8 and SOLO-D2.8. Of the 6,436 D2.8 cells absent from H33, 3,891 were removed in the D2.8-to-SOLO step at the immediate catalogue flank, and 2,545 more in the SOLO-to-H33 step. The removed cells are within two 100-m pixels of the mapped catalogue; H33 has no dots within that two-pixel collar in this audit.
- H33 is an H19-5-derived ridge-network prediction. Its distinction is a thinning/pruning rule, not an independent geological measurement of a new fault.

Sources: [`docs/data/h33_lineage_20261028_a896e29e.json`](../docs/data/h33_lineage_20261028_a896e29e.json), [`docs/data/h33_collar_20261028_a896e29e.json`](../docs/data/h33_collar_20261028_a896e29e.json), and the pinned nine-site audit [`docs/data/focus_sites_20261026.json`](../docs/data/focus_sites_20261026.json).

### Mechanistic interpretation, explicitly uncertain

A plausible explanation for H33's **USER-REPORTED** increase over D2.8 is that a spaced ridge network retains broad spatial coverage while removing dots that are crowded close to known catalogue traces. The competition's distance-weighted Tversky objective uses a 300-m triangular kernel at the 100-m grid, so predictions within the kernel can share coverage neighborhoods. Removing redundant predictions could change false-positive mass and truth coverage; spacing could reduce over-concentration. The actual effect depends on hidden truth and the separate `TP_w`, `FP_w`, and `FN_w` terms. The shared metric code defines `DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + 0.2·FP_w + 0.8·FN_w)`: thinning reduces predicted mass but can also lose recall, and the relatively larger FN coefficient means “fewer dots” is not automatically better.

This mechanism is **not proven to have caused** the reported `0.2477 → 0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778` progression. Those are user-supplied historical values from sequential variants, not a randomized, paired experiment on hidden labels. No organizer receipt or hidden-truth map is present here. A nearby new fault is not ruled out simply because a point is near a public catalogue trace; catalogue masking is pixel-exact, while the metric has a 300-m distance kernel. This makes removal of the collar a plausible precision/coverage tradeoff, not proof that every removed point was a false positive.

The next H33-related variant at GEMSDOE36 adds 1,200 cells yet has a lower user-reported value (0.2750). That observation is consistent with a possible over-emission penalty, but different file generation, score context, or other effects are confounded; it cannot establish a causal marginal effect.
