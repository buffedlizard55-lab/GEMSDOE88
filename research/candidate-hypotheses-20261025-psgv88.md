### 1.2 Exact Metric Algebra of the DrivenData DOE GEMS Challenge

On the $3730 \times 3292$ `EPSG:32611` ($100\text{ m}$ pixel) grid (`12,279,160` total cells; `5,167,373` active footprint cells; `60,988` mapped catalogue fault cells in `existing_faults.tif`, leaving `5,106,385` off-catalogue footprint cells `[DISK]`), let $G \subset \Omega \setminus C$ denote the hidden test fault pixels (with known mapped faults $C = \text{existing\_faults.tif}$ masked to zero before scoring), and let $S = \{p \in \Omega \setminus C : \hat{y}(p) > 0\}$ denote a binary $\{0, 1\}$ submission of cardinality $n = |S|$.

Define the 300 m ($R = 3\text{ px}$) radial triangular distance-buffered envelopes (`vendor/gems57/metric.py`, verified against the [official DrivenData GEMS reference solution](https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution/tree/aebe92f7c8a990f0e3443451b7a825d9afd6336b)):
$$w_A(x) = \max\Bigl(0,\, 1 - \frac{\min_{a \in A} \|x - a\|_2}{3\text{ px}}\Bigr), \qquad A \in \{S, G\}.$$
Then the true-positive recall mass $T(S, G)$, matched prediction mass $M(S, G)$, false-negative mass $\text{FN}(S, G)$, and false-positive mass $\text{FP}(S, G)$ are:
$$T(S, G) = \sum_{g \in G} w_S(g), \qquad M(S, G) = \sum_{s \in S} w_G(s), \qquad \text{FN}(S, G) = |G| - T(S, G), \qquad \text{FP}(S, G) = n - M(S, G).$$
With $\alpha = 0.2$ (false-positive weight) and $\beta = 0.8$ (false-negative weight), the Distance-Weighted Tversky Index is:
$$\text{DTI}(S, G) = \frac{T(S, G)}{T(S, G) + 0.8\,\text{FN}(S, G) + 0.2\,\text{FP}(S, G)} = \frac{T(S, G)}{0.8\,|G| + 0.2\bigl(T(S, G) + n - M(S, G)\bigr)}.$$

Taking the finite difference when adding or removing a pixel $p$ from $S$ exposes three structural regimes that govern every submission on the Great Basin grid:

1. **The Submodular Saturation / Redundancy Penalty Along Predicted Ridges:**
   Because $w_S(g) = \max_{s \in S} \max(0, 1 - \|g - s\|_2 / 3)$ is a pointwise maximum over the 29-pixel disk of radius $R = 3\text{ px}$, a second predicted dot $p'$ placed within $1\text{–}2\text{ px}$ of an existing dot $p$ contributes diminishing marginal recall $\Delta T(p' \mid S) \ll \Delta T(p \mid \emptyset)$, while every off-fault dot still incurs the full linear false-positive denominator penalty $+0.2\,(1 - w_G(p'))$. Removing a redundant dot improves DTI whenever:
   $$\Delta T < \frac{0.2\,\text{DTI}}{1 - 0.2\,\text{DTI}}\,(1 - \Delta M) \approx 0.0588\,(1 - \Delta M) \quad (\text{at }\text{DTI} \approx 0.2778).$$
