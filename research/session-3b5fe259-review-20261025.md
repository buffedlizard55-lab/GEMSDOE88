For binary prediction dots P, hidden fault pixels G, and local triangular credit `k(d) = max(1 − d/300m, 0)`, define `T = Σ_{g∈G} max_{p∈P} k(d(g,p))` and `M = Σ_{p∈P} max_{g∈G} k(d(g,p))`. The **local shared evaluator's** alpha=0.2, beta=0.8 formula is `DTI = T / [0.8|G| + 0.2(T + |P| − M)]` (`vendor/gems57/metric.py`, exercised against its brute-force implementation in `tests/test_dti_algebra.py`). Faults already in the public catalogue are masked in the competition scenario; buffered hide-and-recover must mask **visible** faults only. Adjacent predictions compete for the *maximum* truth credit but each can add false-positive mass. Spacing dots around one 300 m kernel radius and deleting low-marginal-credit dots can improve the precision/coverage trade-off. A deleted dot with marginal coverage `δT` and own truth credit `q` improves DTI *iff* `δT(1 − 0.2·DTI) < 0.2·DTI(1 − q)`; a pure false positive (`δT=q=0`) always helps. The observed near-catalogue pruning is **consistent** with that mechanism. Neither the hidden fault geometry nor the individual credit of removed dots is known, so this does **not** prove they were false positives, infer a vent location, or explain the entire user-reported result.

**Scientific target:** the competition predicts *previously unmapped geological faults indicative of geothermal resources*, not a map of confirmed geothermal vents. Cover-softened scarps, magnetic marker offsets, hydrothermal alteration and conductivity can all have non-fault causes. Prospective geological validation must therefore include structural falsification and a buffered whole-component holdout; a high holdout score on known faults is **not** a projection of the hidden-label score.
