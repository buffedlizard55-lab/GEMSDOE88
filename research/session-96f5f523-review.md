**Mechanistic explanation, conditional rather than proven:** under the existing shared DTI definition, truth credit uses a maximum across nearby predictions, whereas false-positive mass accumulates per prediction. Sparse placement can avoid redundant mass; deleting low-marginal-credit dots can improve the ratio without discovering any new structure. Pixel-exact known-fault masking can leave a near-catalogue zone with unfavorable marginal utility. However, an extra collar can also remove valid nearby undiscovered faults. A fixed output using the full catalogue cannot establish its own recovery ability by subsequently hiding catalogue segments. Neither the pruning mechanism nor the user-reported score proves H33 outperformed all other geological methods causally.

For binary predictions the shared algebra keeps truth credit `T` and dot self-credit `M` separate: denominator `D = 0.2*(T+n-M) + 0.8*K`. They cannot generally be collapsed to `0.2*n + 0.8*K`. A proposed additional dot is useful only if its marginal truth credit outweighs its incremental denominator. Hidden truth is unavailable, so this inequality is explanatory, not a prescription that guarantees an organizer score.

**Can we beat H33?** Conceivably, by finding genuinely new high-marginal-credit structures rather than repeatedly thinning the same field.
