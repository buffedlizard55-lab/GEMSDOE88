## 2. Why H33 (`GEMSDOE32`, `0.2778`) may have ranked well—and what is not proven

1. **It appears to be a selective subtraction descendant, not a new broad predictor.** The saved support lineage is nested (`H19-5` → `D2.8` → `SOLO-D2.8` → H33). The earlier registry audit measured that H33 removed `2,545` of its immediate SOLO parent dots; comparing D2.8 directly to H33 gives `6,436` removed dots, all within 2 pixels of the known catalogue. These are **REGISTRY-MEASUREMENTS** of saved raster geometry, not fault truth.
2. **The metric makes precision-focused thinning plausible.** Under the stated distance-weighted Tversky setup, duplicate/redundant predictions around known mapped structure can contribute false-positive weight while the 300 m kernel means nearby predictions may claim overlapping credit. Removing a collar of suspicious dots and suppressing tightly clustered emissions can improve the precision/coverage trade-off if those dots do not add unique hidden-fault coverage.
3. **But subtraction can also erase real undiscovered structure.** Catalogue proximity is not ground truth for whether a location is a false positive. The geometric mechanism is consistent with a gain, but there is no causal ablation against the competition labels and no receipt in the repository establishing that H33's public claim is authentic or reproducible.

Thus the defensible answer to “can we beat 0.2778?” is: **possible in principle, not demonstrated here**. We cannot claim a higher score based on local holdout numbers or projections.
